# Napoleon's Russian campaign, 1812

*Minard's map, rebuilt from his numbers, with the battles as notes.*

> [!stats]
> - **422,000** crossed the Niemen
> - **100,000** reached Moscow
> - **10,000** came back
> - **−38 °C** on December 6

Charles Joseph Minard drew this in 1869: the band is the army, its width the number of men, gold going east and black coming back, with the temperature on the retreat underneath. Edward Tufte called it about the best statistical graphic ever drawn. Here the army and the temperatures come from Minard's own figures (the HistData tables); the battles are notes in this folder, so adding one puts it on the map.

![[Campaign 1812.base#Minard map]]

## Reading the map

- Barely one soldier in forty came back. Most were lost to hunger, disease and desertion before the first snow, not in battle.
- Most of the loss happens in summer: the main column is down from 340,000 to 145,000 by Smolensk, months before the first frost.
- The cold arrives with the retreat. The worst readings, at the Berezina and after, line up with the band thinning to a thread: see [[Battle of the Berezina]].
